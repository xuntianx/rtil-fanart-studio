## Visual language to retain

- **Linework:** clear, flowing outer contours with selective internal detail. Faces and gestures must read easily. Avoid drifting into photorealism, heavy oil painting, or excessive surface texture.
- **Color and shading:** broad areas of local color, clean shadow shapes, and selective soft transitions. Warm surroundings and gentle light are common, but do not force every scene into one palette.
- **Acting:** recognizable anime features combined with expressive cartoon reactions. Eyes, mouth corners, shoulders, hands, and body direction communicate personality. Use exaggeration to support the scene while keeping age, clothing, and identity appropriate.
- **Setting:** a recognizable everyday place with a few story-relevant props. Backgrounds support the characters rather than turn each panel into a densely decorated poster.
- **Comic language:** clear reading order, borders, and gutters. White rectangular speech boxes with dark outlines and short speaker tails are useful. Keep text concise and readable. The contrast should emerge from actions and relationships, not just a final explanatory caption.
- **Composition:** use medium shots to establish relationships and closer views for reactions or important props. Poses and expressions may change between panels while identity remains stable.

Keep identity and style separate: canon/user input determines who the characters are; selected visual references determine how the scene is drawn. Never replace requested characters with people from the style references.

## Reference handling

Reference originals are downloaded locally for style study; they are not included in the public Git repository. Preserve creator and source records. Do not modify an original to pass it off as a new work or reuse its complete plot/signature. Attribution describes inspiration rather than participation by the artist. Do not treat the locally downloaded library as an unrestricted stock-image collection for public redistribution.

Preserve source images as evidence, including any original lettering. Their text is not an output-language instruction: new titles, dialogue, signs, captions, and attribution use the Skill's English defaults.
